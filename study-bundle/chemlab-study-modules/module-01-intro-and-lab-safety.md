# Module 1: Introduction to Chemistry & Lab Safety

## 1. Why Chemistry Matters for Engineers
Engineering relies on **materials, energy, and processes**, all of which are governed by chemical principles:
- **Materials & Corrosion:** Understanding oxidation and galvanic coupling explains why structural steel rusts, why aluminum passivates, and how sacrificial anodes protect pipelines and hulls.
- **Energy Storage & Conversion:** Battery electrochemistry (lithium-ion, lead-acid), fuel cells (PEMFC), and combustion thermodynamics dictate energy density, efficiency, and degradation.
- **Process & Environmental Safety:** Knowing chemical reactivity, vapor pressure, toxicity, and neutralization prevents runaway reactions, toxic gas release, and environmental contamination.

---

## 2. Laboratory Conduct, Housekeeping & Hygiene
- **Never work alone** in a laboratory without instructor or supervisor authorization.
- **No food, drink, gum, or cosmetics** in the lab; chemicals can transfer from bench surfaces or gloves to hands and mouth.
- **Never pipette by mouth**—always use a rubber bulb or mechanical pipette pump.
- **Wafting technique:** Never inhale vapors directly over a container; gently wave your hand over the opening toward your nose.
- **Acid dilution rule ("Always Add Acid" — AAA):** Always add concentrated acid slowly to water with stirring, never water to concentrated acid, to prevent violent exothermic boiling and splattering.
- **Housekeeping:** Keep aisles clear, wipe spills immediately according to SDS protocols, and wash hands thoroughly with soap and water before leaving the lab.

## 3. Essential Laboratory Glassware & Apparatus

| Equipment | Primary Function | Precision / Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Beaker** | Holding, mixing, and heating liquids | Approximate volume markings only ($\pm 5\%$); never use for accurate volume measurement |
| **Erlenmeyer Flask** | Swirling titrations, heating, reducing splash/evaporation | Conical shape prevents spills during agitation; approximate graduations |
| **Graduated Cylinder** | Measuring general liquid volumes | Moderate precision ($\pm 0.5\text{–}1\%$); read at the bottom of the meniscus at eye level |
| **Volumetric Flask** | Preparing standard solutions of exact concentration | Calibrated to contain (TC) one specific volume at $20^\circ\text{C}$ ($\pm 0.05\%$) |
| **Burette** | Delivering variable, precise liquid volumes in titrations | Calibrated to deliver (TD) to $0.01\text{ mL}$ (read to $0.05\text{ mL}$) |
| **Volumetric Pipette** | Transferring a single fixed, high-precision volume | Highest accuracy for fixed aliquot transfer |
| **Watch Glass** | Covering beakers, evaporating small samples, weighing solids | Curved glass; not heated directly over an open flame |
| **Crucible & Cover** | Heating solids to very high temperatures (ashing, dehydration) | Porcelain or platinum; handle hot crucibles with crucible tongs |
| **Spatula / Scoopula** | Transferring small quantities of solid reagents | Wipe clean between reagents to prevent cross-contamination |
| **Condenser (Liebig)** | Cooling vapors back into liquid during distillation/reflux | Cooling water enters at the **bottom** and exits at the **top** (counter-current flow) |
| **Bunsen Burner & Striker** | Open-flame heating | Adjust air collar for a clean, non-luminous blue inner cone; keep away from flammables |

## 4. Handling, Storing & Labelling Chemicals (GHS Pictograms)
Every reagent container must carry a legible label with chemical identity, concentration, hazard warnings, and date. The **Globally Harmonized System (GHS)** uses nine red-bordered diamond pictograms:

| GHS Pictogram | Hazard Class | Key Safety Rule & Common Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Exploding Bomb** | **Explosive** | Shock-, friction-, spark-, or heat-sensitive; keep away from ignition and impact (e.g., picric acid, organic peroxides) |
| **Flame** | **Flammable** | Ignites easily; keep away from open flames, sparks, and oxidizers (e.g., acetone, ethanol, hexane) |
| **Flame Over Circle** | **Oxidizer** | Releases oxygen or enhances combustion of other materials; store separately from flammables (e.g., $\text{KMnO}_4$, $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$, $\text{HNO}_3$) |
| **Gas Cylinder** | **Compressed Gas** | High-pressure cylinder; strap upright to bench/wall and keep valve cap on when moving (e.g., $\text{N}_2$, $\text{O}_2$, $\text{Ar}$ cylinders) |
| **Corrosion** | **Corrosive** | Destroys skin/eyes on contact and corrodes metals; wear splash goggles, lab coat, and chemical gloves (e.g., $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$, $\text{HCl}$, $\text{NaOH}$) |
| **Skull & Crossbones** | **Acute Toxicity** | Fatal or severely toxic even in small doses via inhalation, ingestion, or skin absorption; use fume hood (e.g., $\text{KCN}$, methanol, $\text{HgCl}_2$) |
| **Health Hazard** | **Serious Health Hazard** | Carcinogen, mutagen, reproductive toxin, or respiratory sensitizer (e.g., benzene, formaldehyde, chromates) |
| **Exclamation Mark** | **Harmful / Irritant** | Skin/eye irritant, narcotic effects, or acute low-level toxicity (e.g., dilute $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{CuCl}_2$, calcium chloride) |
| **Environment** | **Hazardous to Environment** | Toxic to aquatic organisms with long-lasting effects; never pour down the drain (e.g., $\text{CuSO}_4$, $\text{AgNO}_3$, heavy-metal salts) |

## 5. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) & Dress Code
- **Required Safe Lab Attire:**
  1. **Chemical splash goggles** (indirectly vented, ANSI Z87.1) whenever chemicals, glassware, or heat are in use.
  2. **Full-length, long-sleeved lab coat** (buttoned up) to protect skin and clothing from spills.
  3. **Chemical-resistant gloves** (nitrile for general aqueous acids/bases/solvents; butyl/neoprene for specialized organics).
  4. **Long pants** covering legs completely to the ankles.
  5. **Closed-toe, closed-heel shoes** covering the entire foot.
- **Strictly Prohibited Lab Hazards:**
  - **Open-toed shoes or sandals** (expose feet to broken glass and chemical spills).
  - **Shorts or skirts** above the ankle (leave bare legs unprotected).
  - **Loose long hair** (must be tied back securely so it cannot catch fire in a Bunsen burner or dip into reagents).
  - **Dangling jewelry** (necklaces, bracelets, loose rings can snag on apparatus or trap corrosive liquids against skin).
  - **Synthetic / acrylic fingernails** (flammable and porous; can trap solvents underneath).

## 6. Chemical Waste Segregation & Spill Response
Never pour chemical waste down the sink unless explicitly authorized. Segregate waste into incompatible streams:

| Waste Stream | Typical Chemicals | Why Segregation Matters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Acids** | $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$, $\text{HCl}$, $\text{HNO}_3$, $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ | Mixing with bases causes violent exothermic heat; mixing with cyanides/sulfides releases lethal $\text{HCN}$ or $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ gas |
| **Bases (Alkalis)** | $\text{NaOH}$, $\text{KOH}$, $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$ | Reacts violently with acids and corrodes aluminum/glass joints |
| **Organic Solvents** | Acetone, ethanol, hexane, ethyl acetate, chloroform | Flammable; keep away from oxidizers and separate halogenated from non-halogenated solvents |
| **Inorganic / Heavy Metals** | $\text{CuSO}_4$, $\text{AgNO}_3$, $\text{Pb(NO}_3)_2$, $\text{Cr}^{3+}$ | Persistent aquatic toxins; must be collected for heavy-metal precipitation/recovery |
| **Neutral Salts** | $\text{NaCl}$, $\text{KNO}_3$, $\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$ | Non-hazardous aqueous salt solutions collected separately from heavy metals and organics |

### Emergency Response Quick Rules
- **Chemical in eyes:** Flush immediately at the **eyewash station for at least 15 minutes**, holding eyelids open.
- **Large body spill:** Use the **safety shower for at least 15 minutes** and remove contaminated clothing immediately.
- **Small bench acid/base spill:** Alert nearby students, wear PPE, contain with dike, neutralize carefully (sodium bicarbonate for acids; dilute citric acid/sodium bisulfate for bases), and sweep into labeled solid waste.
